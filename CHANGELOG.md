# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [Unreleased]

Principal and identity attribution replace implicit owner authority.
Ordinary single-owner Discord configurations remain accepted, but direct runtime callers, custom background turns, and web integrations must follow [Migrating to 0.9](docs/migration-0.9.md).
The guarantee isolates principal memory exchanges and private conversations, not shared credentials or arbitrary model disclosures in public replies.

### Added

#### Identity and access

- The built-in `identity` plugin, registered first in every host: the tables `principals`, `principal_identities`, and `principal_roles` (ledger ids `identity/principals`, `identity/principals-claimable`, and `identity/principals-plugin-links`), and a backfill that runs at every boot (`identity/backfill`) and makes a principal of the same id for the configured owner and every person id stored in `owner_memory`, `schedules`, `conversations`, and `held_actions` (a held action that names its `principal_id` for the hold it holds is skipped: its `speaker_id` is an actor id; so is any id a linked identity is known by, its subject or `<provider>:<subject>`, since this version wrote that row). A principal it makes may be claimed once by the person of that id, unless it is a configured owner's, which is never claimable, even after the configuration names another owner. 0.8's `remote-mcp` speaker, the owner writing through an outside agent over MCP, gets no principal of its own: it is linked as `legacy:remote-mcp` to the primary owner's principal, so the schedules it created stay the owner's, at the owner tier. A carried-over author of schedules who is not a configured owner is recorded as seen at the upgrade, at the highest tier they scheduled at but at most the highest tier the access rules could give them (never owner), so their schedules keep running for `backgroundStaleDays` until they are seen again. It only inserts, so no existing row changes, and it logs one line with what it made. A test that lists the host's plugins or migrations now finds `identity` and its four migrations first.
- `IDENTITY`, the identity service the `identity` plugin provides: `resolve(facts, { conversation }?)` turns what a surface reports about a person (`ActorFacts`: `provider`, `subject`, `name`, `roles` written `<surface>:role:<name>`, optional `surface`, `space`, and `legacyId`, the 0.8 speaker id) into a `Speaker` with its `principalId`, or undefined when the access rules serve no one by them. A linked identity is its principal's; an unlinked one first claims the principal of its 0.8 id, so a person carried over from 0.8 keeps their memory, schedules, and conversations; a principal the backfill could only name by its id takes the name the facts carry as it is claimed, while one it named, such as from a schedule, keeps that name. A principal is claimed at most once: only one the backfill made, before any identity is linked to it, and never one holding the owner role, so an identity unlinked by the CLI or dropped from the configuration does not claim it back; otherwise someone the rules admit gets a new `p_<ulid>` principal. A disabled principal is no one; the owner tier comes only from the configured owners or a role the CLI grants, never from a role a surface reports. The tier is the higher of the principal's lasting roles and what the rules give the facts on this contact. Someone refused is not claimed or admitted; a linked person refused is recorded as seen at no tier, except by facts that lack the roles the rules decide by, such as a Discord DM, which neither refuse nor lower anyone on record. The service is read-only to plugins, and what it returns is a frozen copy: `principal(id)`, `list()`, `identities(principalId)`, `roles(principalId)`, `tierOf(principalId)` (the tier of the lasting roles), `owners()`, and `speakerFor(principalId, tier?)`, for a turn started on someone's behalf at their tier or lower (the tier last seen for someone whose tier comes only from the rules, refused after `backgroundStaleDays` or once a contact of theirs is refused), complete it. Principals, links, and roles are written only by the configuration and the `roundtable principal` CLI, and `speakerFor` refuses `SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL`, whose turns only the core starts. Reads are reused for 30 seconds, so a change another process makes is seen within that time. At every boot the configured owners are made, linked, and granted the owner role; an owner or an identity the configuration no longer names loses what the configuration gave it, and a configured owner's identity linked to someone else stops the boot with a `ConfigError`. Types: `IdentityService`, `ActorFacts`, `AccessRules`, `AccessOwner`, `AccessTier`, `Principal`, `IdentityLink`, `IdentityRef`, `LinkSource`, `RoleGrant`, `RoleSource`; values: `IDENTITY`, `SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL`, `IdentityError`.
- The `access` configuration key: `access: { owners: [{ name, pronouns?, principal?, identities? }], admins?, members?, provisioning?, backgroundStaleDays? }`. Identities are written `<provider>:<subject>` (`discord:<user id>`, `token:remote-mcp`, and for OpenID Connect `oidc:<issuer>:<subject>`, the web chat's 0.8 speaker id); roles `<surface>:role:<name>`, such as `discord:role:<role id>` or `web:role:App.User`; `everyone` is `true` for every surface or a list of surfaces. The first owner is the primary owner and needs a `principal` in 0.9, such as the old `owner.id`; with Discord configured they also need a `discord:` identity, which the Discord parts act for. `provisioning` is `"admitted"` (default) or `"linked"`, which serves only identities already linked (by the CLI or as a configured owner's) and claims no 0.8 principal; `backgroundStaleDays` defaults to 30. `access` with `owner` or `speakers`, or neither, is a configuration error. `roundtable init` writes `access` in both templates. Types: `AccessConfig`, `AccessOwnerConfig`, `AccessTierConfig`.
#### Operator commands

- `roundtable upgrade [--write]` rewrites `roundtable.config.ts` in the 0.9 form: `owner` and `speakers` as the `access` they mean (the owner's `principal` is the old `owner.id`, their identity `discord:<id>` on a host with Discord and none without; users `discord:<id>`, roles `discord:role:<id>`, `everyone` as `["discord"]`), and the top-level `discord` as `adapters: [discord({ ... })]` with its import. It shows a diff and writes only with `--write`, after loading the rewrite beside the file and checking that the host would serve the same owners, admins, and members with the same Discord settings. The check loads the file as the host does, so it needs the environment the host runs with: when the file does not load as it is, such as with secrets kept outside `.env`, the preview says it was not checked and `--write` refuses (exit 1) until that environment is set; `--write --unchecked` writes it anyway, with a warning. It keeps every comment and says which it moved, changes no other file, and refuses, naming the line, what it cannot read with confidence, such as an owner built elsewhere or a spread configuration. A second run finds nothing to change.
- `roundtable principal list | show | create | link | unlink | grant | revoke | disable | enable` lists and changes the principals, their identities, and their roles in the host's database. `list` prints each identity as `access` and `link` take it, `<provider>:<subject>`, and says of a principal with none whether its 0.8 id still links its own at first contact; `link` says when it uses up that claim, which no later `unlink` gives back; the owner role is granted only here and in the configuration; `grant` of a role the configuration already gives changes nothing and says so, and `revoke` refuses it, so it stays the configuration's to take away; `unlink` refuses an identity the configuration lists under that owner, whoever linked it (the configuration, a first contact, the CLI, or a 0.8 claim), and also when the configuration's schema rejects it, since a running host would admit it as someone new and the next start would fail on it, and says to remove it from `access.owners[*].identities` instead; it takes one the configuration moved to another owner, as a start that stops on it asks, and says to restart the host with that configuration, unlinking again any new `p_…` principal that takes the identity meanwhile. The owners are read by the configuration's schema alone, so a plugin or model runtime that fails does not pass for a configuration that does not load. A `<principal>` that is both a principal's id and an identity linked to another principal, as a 0.8 web user's id can be, is refused as ambiguous, naming both. A running host sees a change within 30 seconds, which every change says.
- `roundtable doctor` checks `access` (a warning when `everyone: true` admits everyone on every surface of a host with more than one) and the principals: the backfill the next start makes, as a dry run printed as the start logs it, a configured owner's identity linked to another principal, which stops the start, and a disabled owner. A warning, printed with `!`, does not fail the doctor.
#### Plugin and conversation contracts

- `RoundtablePlugin.identities`: identities a plugin's own credentials stand for, each a `PluginIdentity` (`{ identity, principal? }`, the identity written `token:<subject>`, by default the primary owner's; only the `token` provider is accepted, since a surface's identity such as `discord:` or `oidc:` is a person's own account, linked by `access.owners` or the CLI). At every start the `identity` plugin links each one to its principal under the new link source `plugin` (ledger id `identity/principals-plugin-links`), moves one bound to another principal than at the last start, and unlinks one no plugin declares any more; an identity that does not parse or is not a `token:` identity, one two plugins declare, the system principal, a principal that does not exist, or an identity linked to someone else by the configuration, the CLI, or a first contact stops the start with a `ConfigError` that names what to change. A declared identity is never admitted or claimed at a first contact, and its link does not use up the principal's 0.8 claim, since a plugin's credential is not the person's own identity. For the same reason its link never tells which principal a configured owner is: an owner in `access.owners` that lists a declared identity is known by its `principal` or its other identities, and when that is not the principal the plugin binds the identity to on that start (its `principal`, or else the primary owner), or nothing else tells who the owner is, the start stops with a `ConfigError` saying to remove the identity from that owner or bind the plugin to them; `roundtable doctor` reports the same. Whom the identity was bound to at an earlier start does not matter, so binding the plugin to that owner lets the next start through, moving the identity to them. `IDENTITY.principalOf(identity)` reads the principal an identity is linked to. `roundtable principal list` and `show` name the plugin that declares a `plugin` link, or say no plugin declares it any more and the next start unlinks it; `unlink` refuses one a plugin declares, or one it cannot tell because the configuration does not load, and says to change the plugin's options instead.
- `Speaker.principalId`, the principal the speaker is, equal to `id` for a person carried over from 0.8. It is required: a plugin that builds a `Speaker` itself gives it, from `IDENTITY.speakerFor` when it starts a turn for someone. `OWNER_SPEAKER` from `pi-roundtable/testing` has it, and the speakers of `speakerPolicy(map)` have their own id as their principal.
- `InboundMessage.actor`: who wrote the message, as the surface reports them (`ActorFacts`); the Discord surface fills it, with the author's roles as `discord:role:<id>` in a server and none known in a DM, and the web chat with an OpenID user as subject under the provider `oidc:<base64url issuer>`, their roles as `<surface>:role:<name>`, and their 0.8 speaker id as `legacyId`, so a web user the access rules serve claims their 0.8 principal under that provider. A surface that leaves it out has it read from `authorId`, `authorName`, and `authorRoleIds` with its prefix as the provider, with a `deprecated` warning once per surface; that goes away in 1.0.
- `InboundMessage.speaker`: who the author is to the host, set by the router alone before any claim admits the message, through `IDENTITY`: undefined when the access rules serve no one by them, when they cannot be resolved (logged), and for bots and integrations, which are not resolved; a surface's own value is dropped. The author is recorded, such as linking them at their first contact or recording them as seen, only once a claim admits the message, so messages no claim takes link or make no one (an already linked author the access rules refuse is still recorded as seen at no tier as the message is resolved, at most once every few minutes), and what a claim admitted runs even when that record fails; a channel's messages still reach their claim in the order they came. `testPlugin` resolves authors through a given `IDENTITY`, and otherwise only the `owner` is anyone, at the owner tier.
- `CONVERSATIONS.adopt(key, principalId)` makes a conversation recorded `shared` with no principal, as 0.8 recorded every turn run without `conversation`, private to that principal, for a plugin migrating what its 0.8 version left, such as remote-mcp's sessions; it never changes a private conversation or one with a principal, is safe to repeat and to race, and resolves what is stored (undefined for an unknown key). A turn's registration still never changes what the first one recorded. A stand-in `ConversationRegistry` needs the method too.
- `Prompts`, `PromptScope`, and `promptScope`: a running turn's prompts are answered by those its scope names, made with `promptScope(speaker, visibility)`: `principalId`, `speakerId`, `tier`, and `escalate`, `owners` in a shared conversation and `none` in a private one. The speaker answers their own question and approves a call at its `minTier`; where the scope escalates to the owners every owner may answer too, and a call above the speaker's tier is theirs alone; where it escalates to no one, such a call resolves `expired` at once, shown to no one. `ChatSurface.prompts(channel, scope?)` receives the scope; `SurfacePort.prompts` and `RuntimeDeps.prompts` take it, and still take 0.8's `Speaker`, read as the speaker of a shared conversation, with a deprecation warning once per host until 1.0. `OwnerPrompts` is a deprecated alias of `Prompts` until 1.0.
- `TurnRequest.conversation` (`TurnConversation`): who the turn's conversation belongs to, `private` to a `principalId` or `shared`; `context.turns` gives it as the registry keeps it, so a private conversation's prompts stay its person's at every turn. The Pi runtime scopes an interactive turn's prompts by it.
- `PendingConfirmation.principalId`: the principal of the speaker whose turn held the calls, stored with them in `held_actions.principal_id` beside the hold it belongs to, `principal_held_at` (ledger id `runtime/held-actions-principal`), so a confirming message from another identity of theirs approves them after a restart too; calls held before 0.9, or held by 0.8 over this version's after a rollback, are matched by `speakerId`.
- `describeSurfaceContract` checks two more things: in a private conversation an owner, another principal, cannot answer for the person (given `SurfaceContractSubject.owner`, a `SurfaceContractAnswerer` like `stranger`), and a private conversation's approval above a speaker below the owner tier resolves `expired` at once without being shown. The example `FakeSurface` keeps its prompts open until someone they are for answers them, with `answer(id, who, approved)`, and runs the contract.

- `CommandGuard.allows(actor)`, a promise of whether the actor is an owner now: the primary owner by user id, and every other owner principal by their Discord identity, checked through `IDENTITY` each time, and a stranger never resolved. `commandGuard` takes `identity` for it, and `DiscordActor` is what it accepts: an interaction, or anything with `user.id`.

#### Personal background work and delivery

- `PERSONAL_TARGET`, the host's own background target: every person's conversations whose claim answers background turns. The core's `modules` plugin contributes it on every host, with or without Discord, where 0.8's agent server contributed it, so a plugin's conversations on a host without Discord schedule and delegate too once its claim answers background turns. Its name stays `"owner"`, as 0.8 stored it, and its limits are 0.8's. `OWNER_TARGET` is the same target, deprecated until 1.0; a plugin that contributes a target named `"owner"` itself now clashes with it.
- Per-person limits: `BackgroundTarget.schedules.perPrincipal` holds each person to that many of a target's schedules across all their conversations (0.8's `remote-mcp` schedules count as the primary owner's), and `BackgroundTarget.delegation.maxRunningPerPrincipal` to that many delegated tasks running at once. Both are optional, and `PERSONAL_TARGET` sets neither: the configuration's new `background: { perPrincipal: { schedules?, delegations? } }` (`BackgroundConfig`, `PerPrincipalLimits`) sets them on the host's personal target, each a whole number from 1, and left out they stay unset, as in 0.8. A host that serves many people sets both. `ScheduleToolContext` (`pi-roundtable/kit`) takes `principalOf`, which tells the principal a creator id stands for, `visibility`, and a store with `all` and `createWithin` where the target counts per person. `ScheduleStore.createWithin(schedule, { creators, max })` counts a person's schedules and stores the new one under a lock on the creator, so creates at once in many conversations stay within `perPrincipal`; a stand-in `ScheduleStore` needs the method too.
- Direct channels: `Contribution.directChannels` takes a `DirectChannelProvider` (`{ name, label, reaches(principalId), deliver? }`), a way to reach a person on their own, such as a chat network's direct messages or a web inbox; two of one name are a `PluginError` naming both plugins. `context.directChannels` (`DirectChannels`) reaches a person through the first provider, in contribution order, that reaches them: `providers()`, `reach(principalId)` (a `DirectReach`, or undefined), and `notify(principalId, text)`, false when none reaches them; it is linked once every plugin is set up, as `conversations` is. The Discord plugin contributes `discord`, which reaches a principal's direct messages through a Discord identity linked to them, the primary owner's configured one first, so a single owner is reached in the same direct messages as in 0.8. `Notifier` (`pi-roundtable/kit`) is what sends such a notice; `OwnerNotifier`, which reaches the primary owner alone, is deprecated until 1.0.
#### Session scope and system reports

- [Migrating to 0.9](docs/migration-0.9.md), including rollback and exact prompt-snapshot differences, and a tested `examples/principals.ts` in the plugin guide's new Principals and access chapter.
- `SessionContext.conversation` (`SessionConversation`): whom a session's conversation serves, fixed when the session is made: `private` to a `principalId`, with the `principal` when the host knows them, or `shared`. An agent's session is shared; another is as `TurnRequest.conversation` names it, else as the conversation registry recorded it, else shared, never the owner's by default. The Pi runtime reads the registry and `IDENTITY` for it. A session's history records whom it served: when the conversation comes to serve someone else (another principal, or private where it was shared, or shared where it was private), the open session is dropped with the actions held in it and its history archived, as `startFresh` does, so the new session replays none of it, after a restart too; a history from before this version, which records no one, carries on. Changed tools or skills still rebuild a session with its history. `runtime.recentTranscript` reads a conversation's history without opening a session, so reading it before a turn decides nothing, and gives none recorded for someone else than the conversation serves now, nor an open session's made for someone else. `SessionContext.addressee` is whom tool descriptions name, and `SessionContext.memory` is `"none"` for a persona that declares `Persona.memory: "none"` (`"speaker"` by default), whose conversations then load no memory tools and no memory in the prompt; `LinkedSessions.personaMemory(kind)` reads it. A plugin or test that builds a `SessionContext` itself gives the three.
- `ChannelClaim.takesSystemReports`, optional and true when left out: a claim whose conversations are each private to the person who opened them, and so refuse the host's own turns (`SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL`), says `false`, and a host whose `ops.conversation` names a conversation it owns does not start, with a `ConfigError` saying its conversations take no system error reports and to name a shared conversation or use `ops.agent`, instead of logging every report as unanswered. `ConversationPort.takesSystemReports(channel)` reads it; a stand-in `ConversationPort` needs the method too.

### Changed

- Official package integrations now use core identity: the web console requires a linked owner identity (an unlinked actor receives 403, even with one owner; `legacy:` is refused; old actor-less verifiers retain warned primary-owner compatibility); webchat removes its `access`, `webAccess`, and `WebAccessMap` in favor of top-level `access`, principal-owned quotas/connections, personal background turns, and a private notice inbox (`notice` frames and REST list/read endpoints), while keeping protocol v1 and refusing system report destinations through `takesSystemReports: false`; remote MCP's token binds to the primary owner by default or an explicit principal and adopts its 0.8 sessions; coding's extra owner skills require a private principal holding owner and are rechecked on execution; sandbox ignores authors without a resolved tier and requires `SandboxSpeaker.principalId` at its runtime, without changing the container protocol or channel-shared memory contract.
- Prompt snapshot compatibility with 0.8 is explicit: only (f) is byte-identical as a whole; (a)/(b)/(c) change only the notify tool name, (e) refuses the missing-speaker turn, and (d)/(g) replace owner wording with "the speaker" (with the per-speaker memory sentence added to (d)'s search/remove tools).
- `notify_owner` is now `notify`, and it notifies the person a conversation serves instead of the primary owner: the conversation's person in a private conversation, the turn's speaker in a shared one, through their direct channel, refusing when none reaches them; the host's own turns, such as a report's, still notify the primary owner. A session has it only when a plugin contributes a direct channel, and a private conversation only when one reaches its person. With only Discord its description is word for word 0.8's, so snapshots (a), (b), and (c) differ from 0.8 only in the tool's name, and the notice reaches the same direct messages. Until 1.0, `notify_owner` in a selection or a profile selects `notify`, a task's `exclude` and a plugin's `requiredTools` naming `notify_owner` name `notify`, and an operator's or a plugin's `toolTiers` entry for `notify_owner` applies to `notify` unless `notify` has its own; using the old name warns once per logger.
- `owner` and `speakers` are deprecated and go away in 0.10: they keep working, converted to the same `access` (the owner's principal is the old `owner.id`, which is also their Discord identity on a host with Discord, and `everyone` means everyone on Discord), so every author keeps the tier 0.8 gave them, and the host logs one `deprecated` warning. `owner` is now optional, required only without `access`.
- `speakerPolicy(map)` now evaluates the access rules its map means on one surface; who it admits, and at which tier, is unchanged.
- Who may talk to the agents, and who besides the owner may answer an approval card, now comes from `IDENTITY` alone, as resolved by the router, instead of a policy of their own read from the configuration: an owner the CLI granted is an owner there too. Who may speak on Discord, at which tier, is otherwise unchanged.
- A private conversation `context.turns` records is its speaker's principal's (`speaker.principalId`), not their surface id's; for a person carried over from 0.8 the two are the same.
- `context.turns.run` refuses, before the turn starts, a turn in a conversation recorded private to another principal than the speaker's; only its person and the host's own turns (`SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL`) speak in it. `notify` in such a turn refuses too, so a speaker's text never reaches the conversation's person.
- Memory is each principal's, never inferred from the tier: a private conversation reads and changes its person's memory, whoever speaks in it; a shared one each turn's speaker's (`speaker.principalId`, where 0.8 read the surface id); and the host's own turns (`SYSTEM_PRINCIPAL`) in a shared conversation read none, their memory tools answering so. A second owner, a web user at the owner tier, or an owner-tier remote MCP token bound to someone else reads their own, where 0.8 gave every owner-tier speaker the owner's memory. The primary owner's 0.8 rows still load: their principal is 0.8's owner id, and remote-mcp bound to them runs as that principal in a conversation private to them.
- A shared conversation's model requests carry the memory of the turn's reader alone, never another person's from its history. A memory tool's result records whose memory it holds (`details.privateTo`), and before each request the Pi runtime replaces a result of anyone else, in the host's own turns everyone's, with `(another person's private memory, hidden)`, and a memory result that does not say whose with a placeholder too, one recorded before this version or a failed call's, such as one whose arguments were refused, while a memory tool whose store fails records whose memory the call was for, so its person still reads it; the call such a result answers keeps its id and name but sends its arguments as `{ hidden: <the same placeholder> }`, in requests and in compaction summaries alike, and `memory_remove`'s answer that nothing matched, which repeats its text, records whose memory it searched too; the prompt states in the history collapse into one leading message of the current prompt, so a provider that keeps mid-conversation system messages no longer receives an earlier speaker's memory section, and the answers of earlier turns go without their reasoning (thinking, encrypted reasoning, tool calls' thought signatures, and the thought signature Gemini puts on an answer's text; OpenAI's signature of an answer's message, which names its id and phase, is no reasoning, but the message and call item ids OpenAI's Responses paired with the removed reasoning go with it, while each call keeps the id its result answers and each message its phase), which may restate the memory their turn read; the running turn keeps its own. A shared conversation's compaction summarizes its history without anyone's private memory, the prompt states, or the reasoning: the core hands the compaction extension placed through `session.compaction.wrap` the history as `summaryProjection` gives it, and Pi's own summary reads the same. `CompactionTiers.wrapCompactor` takes a third `prepare` argument for it, and `pi-roundtable/kit` exports `summaryProjection` and `privateCompaction`; a compactor of your own that reads a shared conversation's history otherwise must call `privateCompaction(event.preparation)` first. What people wrote and the assistant's replies stay shared, and a private conversation's requests are unchanged: the guarantee covers memory exchanges, a memory tool's call and result or a call whose worker loaded the reader's memory and its result, and earlier turns' reasoning, while anything else the model writes, into another tool's arguments or its reply, is public. A tool of your own whose result holds someone's memory returns the same `details`. A tool whose `session.runTask` worker loaded the reader's memory records its result as theirs the same way, and so do the calls it runs within, a nested call's outer call or the call whose worker started it, since the worker's report may repeat that memory; a call merely running beside it, its result public, is not.
- A host that keeps memory (the default) refuses to start on a model of the `claude-bridge` provider when several people may speak in its shared conversations: more than one owner, `access.admins` or `access.members` that admit anyone besides an owner's own identities listed again, a plugin's identity bound to another principal than the primary owner's, or a principal besides the primary owner holding a role stored by `roundtable principal grant`, read at the preflight and again before every turn of a shared conversation. claude-bridge resumes the history Claude Code stored for a conversation, unfiltered, so the core's hiding of each person's private memory does not reach it. The `ConfigError` says why and how to fix it: a model of another provider, `memory: false`, or one person alone. `roundtable doctor` reports it under the model check. Once the host runs, a role granted to someone else, or an agent switched to a claude-bridge model, refuses the next shared-conversation turn on claude-bridge, the host's model or an agent's, with the same reason before the model is asked. A single owner's host on claude-bridge starts as before.
- Tool descriptions name whom a conversation serves. In a private conversation the memory tools, `ask_user`, and `notify` name its person by their name and pronouns, so a single owner's own conversations read exactly as in 0.8 and a web member's no longer name the owner; in a shared conversation, such as a room a member speaks in, the memory tools and `ask_user` name "the speaker" and say each speaker has a memory of their own, as an agent's already did. `notify` there names the primary owner while only they can receive its notice (no other owner, and only owners may use it), and "the speaker" otherwise, read again before each turn, so an open session follows an owner granted or revoked meanwhile. A turn's approvals and answers name a second owner by their name, not the primary owner's. A conversation 0.8 recorded shared stays shared, its wording now the speaker's; a plugin whose 0.8 conversation was one person's adopts it.
- The Pi runtime refuses a turn, before the model is asked, in a conversation private to another principal than the speaker's, unless it is the host's own turn, as `context.turns.run` does; a plugin calling `runtime.runTurn` directly gets `{ ok: false, error }`. At every turn it reads whom the conversation serves: as the turn names it, else as the conversation registry records it then, else as its open session was made for or its history records, so a conversation nothing names carries on as it was after a restart, and so a conversation adopted as someone's private one while its session is open refuses everyone else's next turn, and that turn's prompts are scoped by it too.
- `IDENTITY` holds someone served and refused by turns, such as by the roles of two Discord servers, at no tier for their background turns for five minutes, instead of writing them as seen at every message.
- `IDENTITY` does not admit or claim, as someone new, an identity the configuration lists under an owner while it is unlinked, such as by another process; it is no one until a start links it again. An identity the configuration lists under an owner that was linked to them another way, such as by the CLI or a 0.8 claim, becomes the configuration's at the next start, so removing it from the configuration unlinks it.
- Discord approval and question cards follow the turn's `PromptScope`. In a shared conversation every owner with a Discord identity may answer them besides the primary owner, so a second owner approves their own owner-tier call, and a card above the speaker's tier mentions every such owner in a thread; in a private conversation only its person may, and a call above their tier expires at once without a card. A principal answers their own card from any of their Discord identities (that the identity is still theirs, and their tier, are checked again when they press, so one moved or unlinked while the card is open answers it no longer and is not resolved); the card mentions the identity that spoke, or all of theirs when the speaker is none of them, and goes to the owners when they have none. An owner-tier call is the speaker's own only when their principal is an owner, so a turn whose tier defaulted to owner asks the owners, as in 0.8. A single owner's cards read and answer exactly as in 0.8. Agent channels approve a held call by message from the principal whose turn held it on any identity of theirs, or an owner. `ChatSurface.prompts` now receives a `PromptScope` instead of a `Speaker`, so a surface of your own reads `scope.speakerId` and `scope.tier` where it read `speaker.id` and `speaker.tier`.

### Removed

**Breaking for 0.1.0, so this is 0.2.0.**
A plugin that still has one of these fields is refused when it is defined or when the host starts, with an error that names the replacement.

- `RoundtablePlugin.useCommands(composed)`: the composed slash commands go to the Discord plugin's surface, not to a plugin. Add commands from `setup` with `context.services.get(DISCORD).commands.add(...)` (see the slash-command entry below).
- `RoundtablePlugin.agentServer()` and the `agentServer(outcome)` event handler, with `AgentServerOutcome`: give a service `startInBackground` and hear how it ended in `serviceStarted`, which names the plugin and service (`AGENT_SERVER_PLUGIN`, `AGENT_TEAM_SERVICE`).
  The host runs every service's background start after all `start`s and the HTTP listeners, without holding up the boot; a failure is logged and heard as `failed`, and does not stop the other services.
- `RoundtablePlugin.stopTurn(channel)`: put `stop(channel)` on the `ChannelClaim` that owns the channel. The router asks only the owning claim, and a claim without `stop` means `false`.
- `PluginContext.core` and its types `CoreAccess`, `CoreServices`, `CoreStores`, `CoreDiscord` and `CoreAgents`: read a service with `context.services.get(KEY)` (`AGENTS`, `SKILLS`, `SCHEDULES`, `MEMORY`, `BACKGROUND_TURNS`, `DELEGATION`, and the Discord entry's `DISCORD`) and provide one with `services.provide`.

  The stores bag is gone: each store belongs to the plugin that owns its table, and the held-action, skill and agent stores are not published (the agent server reads them; `AGENTS.directory` is the read-only half of the agents store).
- The `testPlugin` option `core` and its type `TestCore`: use `services` with `servicePair`.
- Kit: the class types of the built-in services (`AgentStore`, `AgentTeam`, `AvatarStudio`, `BackgroundTurns`, `ConfirmationJudge`, `Delegator`, `OwnerMemoryStore`, `PendingConfirmationStore`, `PiAgentRuntime`, `ScheduleStore`, `SkillRegistry`, `SkillStore`), replaced by the main entry's port interfaces where a plugin needs them, and the `*Options` types of those classes (`AgentTeamOptions`, `AvatarStudioOptions`, `BackgroundTurnsOptions`, `ConfirmationJudgeOptions`, `DelegatorOptions`, `PiAgentRuntimeOptions`, `SkillRegistryOptions`, `TeamTurnsOptions`), with `Detached`, `SkillEntry` and `SkillGroup`.

### Changed

- **Breaking for the unreleased types:** `DefineOverrides` has five fields: `modelRuntime`, `logger`, `errorSink`, `listeners` and `aborted`. `errorReporter` is gone (set `config.ops` for the ops agent's reports, and `errorSink` for your own), `options` is gone (`options.listeners` is `listeners`, which adds listeners to the one `config.http` names, and `options.aborted` is `aborted`; the other host options come from the config), `modules.agentChannelOf` is gone (the modules read `AgentTeam.channelOf` from `AGENTS` when a tool runs), and `agentServer.ownerSessions` is gone (contribute `personas: [{ kind: "owner", prompt }]` and `requiredTools` from the plugin that owns the owner's conversations; the agent server no longer refuses a contributed `"owner"` persona). With no `"owner"` persona the owner's conversations start with an empty system prompt, as before.
- **Breaking for the unreleased types:** `AgentServerOptions`, `ModulesOptions`, `ErrorReporter`, `ErrorReportDelivery` and `ErrorReporterOptions` are no longer public: they were named by `DefineOverrides` only, and the public type-leak baseline is now empty. `Logger` is no longer pino's type, and `LogEntry` moved from `pi-roundtable/kit` to the main entry.
- **Breaking for the unreleased types:** migration names are unique inside a plugin, not across plugins (the ledger id is `<plugin>/<name>`), and `MigrationError.migration` and the start's error name that id. The same name in two plugins is allowed; a repeated id is still refused.
- The upgrade adds the ledger to a database that already ran the migrations without special handling: no id is recorded, so each `once` migration runs once more, as every start did before, and is recorded. **Rollback note:** a build from before the ledger ignores the table and runs every migration at every start; rolling forward again finds its ids recorded, so a `once` migration that fixes rows (for example one that gives rows written before speakers or guilds existed their owner or guild) does not run again over rows an older build wrote in between. Mark such a migration `runs: "every-boot"` if a rollback must converge.
- The host repository's tests use `migrateDatabase` where they migrated by hand, and its two import scripts read the ledger instead of migrating.
- **Breaking for the unreleased types:** the skills are provided by the new `skills` plugin, not by the agent server. the agent server takes no skills option (the paths are the `skills` config key's), the agent server provides only `AGENTS`, and the agent team reads the registry with `find(SKILLS)`, so the skill tools are the `skills` plugin's agent session tools (`skill-tools`) and its `agentSelection`, no longer part of `agent-tools`. `memoryPlugin` takes the `owner` and adds the `owner-memory` session tool, and `modulesPlugin` no longer adds that tool or `discord-admin`, which is the new plugin's.
- **Breaking for the unreleased types:** the core's tier table (`CORE_TOOL_TIERS`) names only `ask_user`, `compact_session` and `read_attachment`; the agent, schedule, delegation, web, skill and memory tools are declared by the plugin that adds them, with the same tiers. The Discord tools stay with the owner, as before.

The stored data, the tool set, and each tool's tier under the default configuration are unchanged (a test records them for the owner's session, an agent's, and a group seat).

- **Breaking for a plugin that named it:** the built-in plugin list of `defineRoundtable` gains `discord-admin` (after `modules`) and `skills` (after it, before `agent-server`).

### Fixed

- `defineRoundtable` no longer sets the process's locale, time zone, or `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`; `Roundtable.run()` applies the host's `environment`, so defining a second host no longer changes the first's.
- The root command `defineRoundtable` gives the host is built when `run()` applies the environment, so its description is in the host's language; `RoundtableOptions.commands.root` may be a `CommandRoot` or a function that returns one.
- One host runs per process: a second `run()` is refused with the fix. A failed `run()` stops what it started, closes the pool, and rethrows, and the same host may retry (tool tiers are declared idempotently per plugin).
- The shutdown drain waits on the host's own channel queue, not only on services, and the Discord built-in no longer contributes a `channel-queue` service.
- `shutdown()` runs once however many signals arrive and returns the exit code (non-zero when something did not stop or the boot had failed); only `listen()` exits the process, and the command line exits non-zero on a failed start.
- Importing `pi-roundtable/testing` in CI without a database URL no longer throws; repository CI requires its PostgreSQL URL in the workflow instead.
- Record the already-published main/testing API and CLI under 0.1.0 rather than presenting them as unreleased additions.

## [0.1.0]

### Added

- Main entry exports: `AgentSeed`, `ChannelClaim`, `Contribution`, `DefinedRoundtable`, `EventHandlers`, `HoldRule`, `InteractionContribution`, `Migration`, `NotLinkedError`, `PluginContext`, `PluginError`, `PromptSection`, `PromptTurn`, `Roundtable`, `RoundtableConfig`, `RoundtableOptions`, `RoundtablePlugin`, `Service`, `SessionTool`, `Speaker`, `Tier`, `ToolContribution`, `ToolRefusal`, `ToolSpec`, `ToolTurn`, `TurnEndEvent`, `TurnEvent`, `definePlugin`, `defineRoundtable`, `defineTool`.
- Testing entry exports: `RecordedEvent`, `TestPluginOptions`, `TestPluginResult`, `testPlugin`.
- The `roundtable` command: `init [dir]` creates a project from `templates/`, `doctor` checks Bun, `.env`, the configuration, PostgreSQL, Discord (token, guild, intents, entry-channel permissions), the model login, and the public URL and says how to fix each failure, `start` runs the checks that need no network and then the bot, and `add plugin <name>` renders `plugins/<name>.ts` and its test and lists the plugin in `roundtable.config.ts`.
- `@babel/parser` as a dependency, for the syntax-tree edit `add plugin` makes to `roundtable.config.ts`.
- `SessionTool.engine`: a compaction-phase tool names the `details.engine` its compactions record, so the compaction tiers tell its compactions from Pi's own. `defineRoundtable` refuses a compactor without one.
- The judge's yes-or-no question is `YesNoQuestion` (`type: "yesno"`), asked with `Judge.askYesNo`, next to `askChoice` and `askScore`.
- `docs/plugins.md`, the plugin guide: what a plugin is, each part with an example, the order things start and stop in, and every startup error with its fix. `examples/` holds one plugin and its test per part; a test fails when an example embedded in the guide differs from its file.
- The README's five-minute path: `init`, `.env`, `doctor`, `start`, a minimal plugin, the requirements, and the `locale` setting.
- `scripts/scan-public.ts` ships in the repository, and CI runs it against the checked-out tree before install, so a host address, Discord id, credential, or private name fails the build. The lockfile may name a registry dependency.

### Changed

- The compaction tiers name the compactor by its role: the engine is `extension` or `pi`, `CompactionTiers` takes the extension's engine as an argument, and its `wrapCompactor` replaces the wrapper named for one compactor.
- Time-zone wording derives from the configured zone in every catalog: English names the IANA id, Traditional Chinese the city, and the `at` and `time` errors of a schedule name the zone.

### Fixed

- `testPlugin`'s `contribution` includes the `agentSelection` a plugin adds; it was left out.
- A setup that throws a `NotLinkedError` reports its reason without a doubled period.
- `pi-web-access` (0.35.0) is a dependency: the built-in delegation worker loads its Pi extension by path, and a project without it failed at the `modules` plugin's setup with `Cannot find module 'pi-web-access/package.json'`.
